Boscos is where modern craft beer in Tennessee started. Tennessee law kept restaurants from brewing and selling their own beer until 1992. When that changed, Boscos opened in the Saddle Creek shopping center in Germantown as the state's first brewpub, and it spent the next decade proving the idea worked.

The Germantown location closed after about ten years, and in 2002 Boscos moved into Overton Square, in the old Bombay Bicycle Club building at 2120 Madison Avenue. The four-sided bar in the middle of the dining room came with the building. It dates back to the Looking Glass, one of the first places to open when Overton Square was developed in the early 1970s.

## What to drink

The year-round lineup includes Flaming Stone, a beer brewed by dropping heated stones into the wort, plus Bombay IPA, Midtown Brown and Isle of Scottish Ale. Boscos also taps a cask for its Mug Club on every Friday the 13th and on St. Patrick's Day. The November 2026 cask was a pre-Prohibition-style lager.

## Good to know

This is a restaurant first, so it's the easiest Memphis brewery to bring a group that doesn't all want beer. Wood-fired pizza, burgers, fish and a Sunday brunch. Reservations are on Resy.
